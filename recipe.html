Koshari

Rice, lentils, pasta, and crispy onions in one bowl.

Why I love this dish

Koshari is a famous Egyptian comfort food. It looks like a lot of work because it is: you end up with four pots and one pan going at once. It is worth it. Every spoonful has soft rice, earthy lentils, tender pasta, chickpeas, a tangy tomato sauce, and a pile of crispy fried onions on top.

At a glance

  • Prep time: 30 minutes
  • Cook time: 1 hour
  • Serves: 4 hungry people

Ingredients

Everything you need for four servings
Ingredient Amount Used for
Brown lentils1 1 cup Lentil layer
Short-grain rice 1 cup Rice layer
Elbow macaroni 1 cup Pasta layer
Vermicelli, broken up 1/2 cup Toasted into the rice
Canned chickpeas, drained 1 can (15 oz) Topping
Large onions, thinly sliced 3 Crispy onion topping
Tomato sauce 1 can (15 oz) Sauce
Garlic cloves, minced 4 Sauce
Ground cumin 2 tsp Sauce and lentils
White vinegar 1/3 cup Tangy garlic vinegar
Also: oil for frying, salt, and chili flakes to taste.

1 Green lentils work too.

Directions

  1. Fry the onions. Cook the sliced onions in plenty of oil over medium heat until deep golden and crisp. Drain on paper towels and save a spoonful of the oil.
  2. Cook the lentils. Simmer them in salted water with a pinch of cumin for about 25 minutes, until tender but not mushy. Drain.
  3. Make the rice. Toast the vermicelli in a little of the onion oil until brown, add the rice, then cover with water and simmer until fluffy.
  4. Boil the pasta. Cook the macaroni according to the package, then drain.
  5. Simmer the sauce. Saute the garlic in oil, add the tomato sauce, cumin, salt, and chili flakes, and let it bubble for 15 minutes.
  6. Assemble. Layer rice, lentils, and pasta in a bowl. Add chickpeas, spoon on the sauce, splash on the vinegar, and finish with a big pile of crispy onions.

Tips and swaps

Do not skip the vinegar. The tang is what ties the whole bowl together.

Shortcut: fry your own onions use store-bought crispy onions on a busy night. It is not the same, but it still tastes good.

Want it spicier?

Stir a spoonful of chili flakes into the tomato sauce, or fry a few chilies with the onions.
